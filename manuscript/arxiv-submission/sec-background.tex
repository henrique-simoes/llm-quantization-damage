\section{Background and related work}\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Weight quantization in the GGUF ecosystem.} llama.cpp \citep{llamacpp} stores weights in
block-quantized ``k-quant'' formats: a tensor is cut into small blocks, each with its own scale, at
nominal widths from 2 to 8 bits. Community builds mix widths per tensor. The Unsloth Dynamic (UD)
builds used here \citep{unsloth_dynamic3,unsloth_qwen38_gguf} keep sensitive tensors at higher
precision and calibrate with an importance matrix, so ``\arm{UD-Q4\_K\_XL}'' names a recipe, not a
uniform 4-bit model. NVIDIA's NVFP4 \citep{nvidia_nvfp4} is a 4-bit floating-point format with
hardware support on Blackwell GPUs; it is served here through vLLM \citep{kwon2023pagedattention,vllm}.

\paragraph{Measuring quantization damage.} The usual report is perplexity on WikiText-2
\citep{merity2017pointer} plus a battery of task benchmarks. Both have known weaknesses. Perplexity is
an average, and practitioners have long noted that the average hides token-level movement
\citep{kalomaze2023ppl}; llama.cpp therefore ships a KL-divergence mode that compares full output
distributions against saved reference logits \citep{llamacpp_perplexity_readme}.
\citet{dutta2024accuracy} made the general argument rigorously: accuracy can stay flat while a large
share of individual answers ``flip'', and they recommend KL divergence and flip counts as the metrics
of record. Public tables by Unsloth \citep{unsloth_dynamic3}, Fireworks
\citep{fireworks2024quantization}, LocalBench \citep{localbench2026gguf} and others
\citep{kurt2026whichquant} report KL divergence, nearly always on prose and at about 2K tokens of
context. Fireworks suggests mean KLD below 0.007 as a deployment-quality threshold; I cite it as an
outside reference point, not as a pass mark. On statistics, I follow \citet{miller2024errorbars} and
\citet{bowyer2025clt}: report intervals, analyse paired designs as paired, and distrust normal
approximations at small $n$.

\paragraph{Long context.} RULER \citep{hsieh2024ruler,ruler_repo} generalises needle-in-a-haystack
tests with multi-key, tracing and aggregation variants. HELMET \citep{yen2025helmet} and NoLiMa
\citep{modarressi2025nolima} show that literal-match retrieval overstates long-context ability.
LongPPL \citep{fang2025longppl} argues that perplexity must be restricted to context-dependent tokens
to mean anything at length. Two studies measure quantization at long context directly and find 4-bit
weights lose measurable accuracy at long context while 8-bit is nearly free
\citep{mekala2025quantlongcontext,redhat_longcontext_quant}. Artificial Analysis' Long Context
Reasoning set (AA-LCR) \citep{artificialanalysis2026aalcr} asks questions that require reasoning over
roughly 100K tokens of real documents. For KV-cache quantization, KIVI \citep{liu2024kivi} and KVQuant
\citep{hooper2024kvquant} establish that 4-bit KV is near-lossless only with per-channel key
quantization, which llama.cpp's block-wise \flag{q4\_0} cache does not do; a community measurement on
a sibling model reports KV-induced divergence one to two orders of magnitude larger at 250K tokens
than at 2K \citep{localbench2026kvquant}.

\paragraph{Speculative decoding.} A cheap drafter proposes several tokens and the target model
verifies them in one pass \citep{leviathan2023speculative,chen2023speculativesampling}. Under exact
arithmetic the accepted output is distributed identically to the target's own. Drafters differ:
Medusa adds extra heads \citep{cai2024medusa}; multi-token prediction (MTP) trains the predictor with
the model \citep{gloeckle2024multitoken,deepseekv3}, and Qwen3.8 ships with such a head; DFlash
\citep{chen2026dflash} is a small block-diffusion drafter that proposes a whole block in parallel and
conditions on hidden states tapped from the target. The Qwen3.8 drafter of that family is referred to
here as DFlash2 \citep{incoai2026dflash2,llamacpp_pr27342_dflash2}. ``Lossless'' holds only up to
floating-point numerics: GPU kernels can be deterministic run-to-run and still give different results
at different batch shapes \citep{thinkingmachines2025nondeterminism}, vLLM documents the same caveat
\citep{vllm_specdecode_docs}, and an open llama.cpp issue reports greedy divergence under
speculation \citep{llamacpp_issue25618}.

\paragraph{Benchmarks used.} HumanEval \citep{chen2021humaneval} with the EvalPlus extended tests
\citep{evalplus}; HellaSwag \citep{zellers2019hellaswag}; SWE-bench \citep{jimenez2024swebench} in its
human-validated Verified subset \citep{openai2024swebenchverified}, driven by mini-SWE-agent
\citep{yang2024sweagent,miniswe}; GPQA Diamond \citep{rein2023gpqa}; RULER; AA-LCR. Qwen's card
reports LiveCodeBench v6 \citep{jain2025livecodebench}, SWE-bench Pro and Terminal-Bench
\citep{merrill2026terminalbench} for this model and no HumanEval figure; none of those three was run
here, so no number in this report is comparable to the model card (\cref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{What is new here, and what is not.} This is a measured case study of one deployment, not
a new evaluation principle. That accuracy can mask distributional change is prior work
\citep{dutta2024accuracy}. That perplexity depends on window, stride and tokenization is documented
\citep{hf_perplexity_guide}. Community tools already report token-level quantiles and tails of KL
divergence alongside the mean \citep{mlx_kld}, including for this model
\citep{malaiwah_kld_method}. Greedy divergence under speculation on quantized targets has been
reported as a bug \citep{llamacpp_issue25618}. What this report contributes are specific measurements
I could not find elsewhere: the code-against-prose comparison of the divergence distribution with a
cache-only control; a paired interval placing a $3.7\times$ divergence increase beside a pass@1
difference within about four points; one 36-fold perplexity swing decomposed into its conventions; a
bracketed context map for a hybrid-attention model on two unlinked consumer cards; a benchmark-scale
count of speculative non-identity with a determinism control; and a same-build MTP versus DFlash2
comparison to 246K tokens of filled context.
