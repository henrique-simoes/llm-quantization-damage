\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Two batteries}
The work came in two batteries plus an earlier exploratory period.

\textbf{Exploratory period\hist{} (2026-08-20 to 08-28).} Perplexity, HumanEval+ in two modes,
SWE-bench Verified, agentic step counts, cross-backend speed. Greedy decoding throughout. Measured on
the deleted build E-H.

\textbf{First battery (08-29 to 09-03), build E-A.} Four builds: \Qfour, \Qfive, \Qsix{} and the
reference \QsixXL. Context ceilings under a layer-split sweep; KL divergence on three corpora;
HellaSwag; paired generative HumanEval+; speculative equivalence and determinism; RULER to 131K.

\textbf{Second battery (09-15 to 09-19), build E-C.} Two builds (\Qfour, \Qsix) $\times$ two drafters
(MTP, DFlash2) on one upstream build: a bracketed context $\times$ KV-precision map, a matched-depth
speed probe, AA-LCR and GPQA Diamond at the model's highest reasoning effort, and a 252K-token
multi-turn soak. Its design was fixed in a decision record before it ran, including the rule for
choosing a drafter.

\subsection{Instruments}
\paragraph{KL divergence.} \texttt{llama-perplexity --kl-divergence} against saved logits from \QsixXL,
context 2{,}048, 32 chunks (65{,}536 tokens) per cell, on (a)~WikiText-2 test, (b)~a Python corpus
built from Django source files, (c)~the 164 HumanEval+ prompts (18{,}432 tokens). The tool reports mean
KLD with a standard error that treats tokens as independent, top-1 agreement, and quantiles of per-token
KLD without intervals. Divergence here is \emph{ladder-relative} (distance from the 6-bit reference,
whose own damage is unmeasured and zero by construction) and is computed on \emph{prompt tokens} under
teacher forcing, so it is not a measurement of generated-code quality.

\paragraph{Task benchmarks.} HellaSwag through the same tool, 400 items, identical items for every
build. HumanEval+ through EvalPlus, 164 problems, scored on base and extended tests. SWE-bench
Verified, 50 instances, mini-SWE-agent with a 250-step limit, scored from per-instance reports on an
x86-64 host. RULER single-needle and multi-key retrieval from RULER's own generators and
\texttt{string\_match\_all} metric. GPQA Diamond (198 items) with Artificial Analysis' prompt template
and last-match answer extraction \citep{artificialanalysis2026methodology}, cross-checked against the
\texttt{simple-evals} extraction pattern \citep{openai_simpleevals}; the two agreed on all 496 scored
answers. AA-LCR (100 questions over 30 document sets of about 100K tokens) with its published
equality-checker prompt, judged by three different LLM judges (\cref{sec:aalcr}).

\paragraph{Context ceiling.} A window counts as reached only if the server (i)~loads with
\flag{-fit off} (otherwise llama.cpp may silently shrink the context), (ii)~reports the requested
\texttt{n\_ctx}, (iii)~prefills at least 94\,\% of it with real source code, and (iv)~then generates a
fixed number of tokens. VRAM is read after the deep prefill, because compute buffers grow with batch;
it is recorded, never used as a pass mark (an early VRAM threshold rejected a rung that works). A
ceiling needs a failed rung above it, attempted twice, because layer-split memory varies by
100--200\,MiB from load to load.

\paragraph{Speed.} Decode rate is the server's own \texttt{predicted\_per\_second} over a generation of
known length. Decode into an empty cache and decode at depth differ by $3$--$5\times$ on this host, so
every speed figure states its filled depth. A harness rule added after a failure
(\cref{sec:corrections}): a speed cell is valid only if the generation reached its requested length.

\subsection{Statistics}
Pass rates carry Wilson 95\,\% intervals \citep{wilson1927interval}. Two builds scored on the same
items are compared as paired data: the difference in proportions $d=(b-c)/n$ over the discordant
counts $b,c$, a Wald interval on $d$, and an exact McNemar test \citep{mcnemar1947test}. Before
reporting a null I state the smallest $p$ that the test could have returned given the number of
discordant pairs; with three discordant pairs that minimum is 0.25, so ``$p=1.0$'' carries no
information about the builds. Continuous summaries use bootstrap intervals \citep{efron1979bootstrap}.
Speed spreads are $(\max-\min)/\text{median}$ over repetitions matched on every launch parameter. The
unit of independence for speculative acceptance is the generation, not the draft event: pooled
per-event intervals of $\pm0.02$ sat on run-to-run ranges of $\pm0.3$. Nineteen tests across the first
battery are corrected together in \cref{sec:multiplicity}.

\subsection{How the work was run}
An agent-driven harness executed the campaign under my direction. I designed the protocol, approved
each experiment before it ran, and am responsible for all content including every error. AI
assistants also helped analyse artifacts and draft this text. The decision log, a ledger of what was
run, and about a hundred per-finding notes (each with its evidence path and caveats) are released with
the data, so the chain from an instruction to a number can be inspected. Independent adversarial
review passes over those notes produced most of the corrections in \cref{sec:corrections}.
Operating rules that earned their place: server logs are saved before any container is removed;
nothing is deleted before its hash is recorded; every battery starts with a pilot that must pass; a
run that measured nothing must not exit successfully; notes are appended and superseded, never edited.
